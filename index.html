What follows is what has actually been built, tested, and deployed — nothing more. --- ## HEADLINE STORY: It Watches. It Never Transmits. Flipperguard is a free, defensive prototype that passively monitors Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) advertisements near your vehicle and alerts you to anomalies. It was built on a single principle: > **Passive monitoring with user permission is the right design.** The tool listens only after you tap Start and allow Bluetooth in Chrome. It never pairs. It never connects. It never jams. It never transmits. --- ## WHAT OUR REPORTER VERIFIED ON THE GROUND **CONFIRMED — Now In The Deployed Build:** - Passive BLE advertisement listening using `navigator.bluetooth.requestLEScan()` - On-screen list of nearby BLE devices — name, ID prefix, RSSI, seen count - Local trusted-device list stored in `localStorage` only, on your phone - Anomaly warning: Unknown + Seen 3+ times + RSSI stronger than -60 dBm = Alert **NOT IN THIS EDITION — And We Will Not Pretend Otherwise:** - No 315 / 433 MHz monitoring in this web build - No RTL-SDR / nRF integration yet - No background monitoring - No server, database, or account system - No pairing, connecting, jamming, or transmitting If you read any other claim elsewhere, it did not come from this version. --- ## HOW TO TEST IT YOURSELF — THE CITIZEN'S GUIDE **Do not take our word for it. Test it.** **You Will Need:** - Chrome on Android — Web Bluetooth is not supported on iOS Safari - Bluetooth switched on - The Live Edition link above, opened over HTTPS **The Test, Step By Step:** 1. **Open** https://topshugar.github.io/Flipperguard/ 2. **Tap** `START PASSIVE SCAN` and allow Bluetooth when Chrome asks. 3. **Wait** 60 seconds. You should see your own nearby BLE devices. 4. **Mark** your phone, watch, and earbuds as Trusted. 5. **Introduce** a second Bluetooth device near the phone for 2 minutes. It should appear as Unknown. 6. **Watch** — if it stays close and is seen 3+ times with strong signal, the alert appears. That is the intended behaviour. 7. **Tap** `Stop` to end the scan. **A Note On Accuracy:** RSSI varies with distance, body, and obstacles. This is an early-warning aid, not a forensic instrument. --- ## THE 315 / 433 MHZ QUESTION — ANSWERED HONESTLY Readers have asked: Does this stop key-fob theft on 315 / 433 MHz? **Answer: Not in this web edition.** That band requires RTL-SDR hardware and a native app. A browser cannot do it. For fob protection today, the practical advice stands: - Use Flipperguard for BLE awareness - Keep your fob in a Faraday pouch --- ## FROM THE ARCHIVE — FILE STRUCTURE No build step. No dependencies.
